Three complementary measurements

eNose: MOS sensor response fingerprint; 15 physical sensors were listed in the instrument software; 12 G-labelled channels are present in the FFT workbook. The mapping between these two naming systems must be confirmed.

GC-MS: volatile compounds in dry powder and brew water; grouped into 6 chemical and 6 aroma categories.

TPC: total phenolic content reported as gallic acid equivalents (GAE).

The GC-MS 6-group workbook covers Hojicha, Hojicha-F, Inome and Inome-F. Another GC-MS workbook contains summary results for tea A–H. Coverage differs by technique.

HS-SPME-GC-MS analysis

องค์ประกอบสารระเหยจากผงชาและน้ำชง พร้อมการจัดกลุ่มเพื่อเปรียบเทียบ aroma profile

Analytical workflow

Technique: Headspace Solid-Phase Microextraction coupled with Gas Chromatography–Mass Spectrometry (HS-SPME-GC-MS).

Library: W10N14.L.

Peak filtering: Match quality ≥ 80; background removal using qualifying Blank CAS matches and general GC background rules.

Reported outputs: Compound, RT, relative %Area, match quality, odour threshold (when available), chemical class and aroma contribution.

GC column / instrument method

The supplied result workbook does not state the GC column model/phase, oven temperature program, carrier gas, injection mode or SPME fiber.

Column details are intentionally left unreported rather than guessed. Confirm from the instrument method or acquisition method file.

Measurement protocol

R10-S120-P60-F200; 7 sampling cycles (about 120 rows/cycle). Blank vial kept as a separate control.

75:25 has only 2 replicates. No third replicate has been duplicated or invented.

What this study can test

  • Whether sensor patterns shift with blend ratio.
  • Whether pure tea measurements form reference points for blend interpretation.
  • Whether regression can estimate blend ratio; report MAE only after validation with held-out independent runs.

The uploaded blend workbook is a raw-data compilation: no baseline correction, filtering, normalization, blank subtraction or PCA has been applied. This page documents the design, not a validated prediction result.

Methods, quality control & data boundaries

eNose preprocessing

  1. Filter Mode = Sampling.
  2. Retain 12 active sensor channels.
  3. Split signal by Cycle Loop; do not concatenate across Rest/Purge.
  4. Linear detrend and mean-center.
  5. Compute rFFT; exclude DC component from power features.
  6. Standardize FFT features before PCA.

GC-MS processing

  1. HS-SPME-GC-MS and library search W10N14.L.
  2. Retain match quality ≥ 80.
  3. Remove background using qualifying Blank CAS matches and listed general contaminants.
  4. Assign compounds to 6 chemical and 6 aroma groups.
  5. Summarize relative %Area; threshold/OAV only where available.

Limitations / metadata to confirm

  • GC column: column model/phase and oven program are not in the supplied result workbook.
  • eNose frequency: FFT workbook assumes 1 sample/s from row sequence; verify raw timestamps before interpreting Hz.
  • Replicate variation: some sensor channels have high between-replicate SD; day/operator effects may be confounded with tea class.
  • PCA: unsupervised visualization, not classification accuracy.
  • Coverage: GC-MS 6-group workbook has 4 samples while eNose has 10 classes; matched integration is limited.
  • TPC: current values are solution concentrations (µg/mL GAE), not normalized to dry tea mass.
